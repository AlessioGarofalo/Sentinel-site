The Entity Model

Most alerts are about the world.
These are about you.

Sentinel is built around a single idea: an event only matters once you can trace how it reaches your own operation. So the model comes first. You describe the company, its sites, and the dependencies that keep them working, and every signal after that is judged against it. A cable cut on the other side of the world is either two steps from your data hall or it is noise, and Sentinel is explicit about which.

STATUS Every provider tells you exactly where it stands
Most tools show a feed as either working or broken. Sentinel distinguishes six states, because "your key is wrong" and "your quota is exhausted" need different actions from you.
OK returned usable articles, with the count
CONFIGURED key present, nothing live on that pass
KEY_REQUIRED missing key, or disabled locally
KEY_REJECTED the provider refused the key, so re-entering it will not help
BACKOFF circuit breaker waiting after quota or rate limits
DEGRADED partial results returned alongside errors

Optional physical feeds stay off until you enable them. Sentinel does not quietly open cameras, broadcasts, or sensor overlays on your behalf.

Sovereign Mode

Your intelligence
stays yours.

Most platforms send your queries, documents, and analysis to cloud servers. For critical infrastructure and crisis work that is a dependency you cannot accept, and your company's dependency map is exactly the document you would least like to hand over. In Sovereign Mode every byte stays on your hardware, and the platform keeps working when the network does not.
